\begin{abstract}
We give a computer-assisted argument that every finite distinct covering
system has a modulus at most $14484$. The distortion measures remain those
of Balister, Bollob\'as, Morris, Sahasrabudhe and Tiba (BBMST).
The improvement replaces a quadratic moment estimate of the
stage loss by a convex comparison with independent prime-adic valuations.
Conditioning on small-prime valuations retains part of the
minimum-modulus restriction. A finite distribution calculation, with explicitly
bounded tails and outward-rounded integer arithmetic, certifies the result.
Floating-point optimization only supplies rational distortion parameters;
it is not used to verify any proof inequality.
The strongest certificate conditions on $85410$ configurations of the first
eight primes, keeps an unrestricted bound outside that finite set, and
tracks true first moments inside bins with graph-based support bounds.
A distribution-stratified fractional-moment tail estimate closes the
infinite calculation, improving the earlier single-moment certificates.
Dynamic conditioning allows nonzero distortions inside the conditioned set,
and a joint product calculation retains forbidden larger-prime divisors
and their dependence on the remaining divisor-count product.
Exact early conditioning and joint optimization of early and later
distortions supply further improvements. A conditioned-complement estimate
also bounds the configurations outside the retained small-prime set.
The target $10000$
remains unproved.
\end{abstract}

\section{Statement and external inputs}
\begin{theorem}\label{thm:main}
If a finite family of arithmetic progressions has distinct positive integer
moduli, all at least $14485$, it does not cover the integers. Consequently
the minimum modulus of every finite distinct covering system is at most
$14484$.
\end{theorem}

The external inputs are the distortion construction of BBMST,
\emph{On the Erd\H{o}s Covering Problem: the density of the uncovered set},
\href{https://arxiv.org/html/1811.03547v1}{arXiv:1811.03547v1}. Numbering here
refers to that version. The earlier certificates use its Theorem 6.1;
the strongest certificate uses the fractional-moment extension proved in
Section~\ref{sec:fractional}, with Dusart's explicit prime-counting bound.
The conditional comparison and all finite distribution
bounds needed for the new calculation are proved below. This is a local
computer-assisted proof, not a proof-assistant formalization or a claim of
independent peer review or publication priority.

Let $p_i$ be the $i$th prime, including absent primes. Choose distortion
parameters $0\leq\delta_i\leq1/2$ and write $\nu_i=(1-\delta_i)^{-1}$.
We use BBMST's stage notation: $A_d=a_d+d\mathbb Z$ is the progression
with modulus $d$, $N_i$ is the set of newly exposed moduli at stage $i$
(those with largest prime factor $p_i$), and
\[
 B_i=\bigcup_{d\in N_i}A_d
\]
is the union of their arithmetic progressions. The distorted probability
measure $\Pp_i$ is constructed inductively from $\Pp_{i-1}$ exactly as
in BBMST. For a fixed earlier-coordinate configuration $x$, write
\[
 F(x)=\{(x,y):y\in\mathbb Z_{p_i^{\gamma_i}}\},\qquad
 \alpha_i(x)=\frac{|F(x)\cap B_i|}{p_i^{\gamma_i}},
\]
where $\gamma_i$ is the exponent of $p_i$ in the common period.
Thus $\alpha_i(x)$ is the proportion of the new fibre covered by $B_i$.
Writing $\E_{i-1}$ for expectation under $\Pp_{i-1}$, the exact loss formula is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:loss}
 \Pp_i(B_i)=\frac{\E_{i-1}\pos{\alpha_i-\delta_i}}{1-\delta_i}.
\end{equation}
The conditional atom bound at an earlier prime $q=p_j$, with exponent
$\gamma_j$ in the common period, is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cap}
 \Pp_{i-1}(X_j=t\mid X_1,\ldots,X_{j-1})
       \leq\frac{\nu_j}{q^{\gamma_j}}.
\end{equation}
It holds at stage $j$ by the fibre construction, and at later stages because
the joint law of every already exposed prefix is preserved.

Set
\[
 C_i=\prod_{j\leq i}\left(1+\frac{\nu_j}{p_j-1}\right),\qquad
 T_i=\prod_{j\leq i}\left(1+
       \frac{(3p_j-1)\nu_j}{(p_j-1)^2}\right),\qquad
 \mu_i=1-\sum_{j\leq i}\Pp_j(B_j).
\]
BBMST Theorem 6.1 states that, for $k\geq10$, the conditions
\begin{equation}\label{eq:stop}
 \mu_k>0,\qquad \frac{T_k}{\mu_k}
 \leq k(\log k+\log\log k-3)^2
\end{equation}
imply noncoverage. Their required unrestricted second-moment bound remains
$M_i^{(2)}\leq T_{i-1}/(p_i-1)^2$ regardless of the stronger estimates used
at earlier stages. Thus the original theorem handles the complete infinite
tail after the verified finite calculation.

\section{Convex comparison, including arbitrary residues}
\begin{lemma}[Nested-event comparison]\label{lem:nested}
Let $A_\ell$ be finitely many events with $\Pp(A_\ell)\leq u_\ell\leq1$,
and let $c_\ell\geq0$. If $U$ is uniform on $[0,1]$, then for every convex
nondecreasing function $\phi$ on the nonnegative reals,
\[
 \E\phi\left(\sum_\ell c_\ell\mathbf1_{A_\ell}\right)
 \leq\E\phi\left(\sum_\ell c_\ell\mathbf1_{U\leq u_\ell}\right).
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Write $S=\sum c_\ell\mathbf1_{A_\ell}$ and $a=\Pp(S>t)$. For $t\geq0$,
\[
 \E\pos{S-t}
 \leq\sum_\ell c_\ell\min(a,u_\ell)-ta
 \leq\max_{0\leq v\leq1}
       \left\{\sum_\ell c_\ell\min(v,u_\ell)-tv\right\}.
\]
If $S^*(u)=\sum c_\ell\mathbf1_{u\leq u_\ell}$, the last expression is
$\max_v\int_0^v(S^*(u)-t)\,du=\int_0^1\pos{S^*(u)-t}\,du$,
because $S^*$ is nonincreasing. The linear case follows from the probability
bounds. Representing a convex nondecreasing function on the bounded range
as a constant, a nonnegative linear term, and a positive integral of
$(x-t)_+$ proves the assertion.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Product comparison]\label{lem:product}
Suppose coordinates $X_j$ obey \eqref{eq:cap}. Let $H_\ell$ be cylinders
specifying one residue modulo $p_j^{a_{\ell j}}$ in coordinate $j$, where
exponent zero imposes no constraint. Let independent $V_j$ have tails
\[
 \Pp(V_j\geq a)=\frac{\nu_j}{p_j^a}\quad(1\leq a\leq\gamma_j),
 \qquad 0\leq V_j\leq\gamma_j.
\]
For $c_\ell\geq0$ and convex nondecreasing $\phi$,
\[
 \E\phi\left(\sum_\ell c_\ell\mathbf1_{H_\ell}\right)
 \leq\E\phi\left(\sum_\ell c_\ell
              \prod_j\mathbf1_{V_j\geq a_{\ell j}}\right).
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Condition on all coordinates preceding the last one. A term whose preceding
constraints fail has coefficient zero. Every remaining last-coordinate
residue event has conditional probability at most $\nu_j/p_j^a$ by
\eqref{eq:cap}; an unconstrained event has probability one. Apply
Lemma~\ref{lem:nested}, using an independent auxiliary variable.
For each fixed value of this variable, the result is again a nonnegative
weighted sum of cylinders in the preceding coordinates. Induction on the
number of coordinates completes the proof. Repeated cylinders and residues
that vary between terms do not affect the argument.
\end{proof}

Since $\nu_j\leq2\leq p_j$, these are valid tail probabilities. We may
extend to independent, unbounded $E_q$ with
\begin{equation}\label{eq:exponent}
 \Pp(E_q=0)=1-\frac{\nu_q}{q},\qquad
 \Pp(E_q=a)=\frac{\nu_q(q-1)}{q^{a+1}}\quad(a\geq1),
\end{equation}
by coupling $V_j=\min(E_{p_j},\gamma_j)$. Adding absent primes and additional
positive terms can only increase the comparison expression.

\section{Keeping the minimum-modulus restriction}
Fix a stage $p=p_i$ and a cutoff $K$. On the preceding smooth numbers put
\[
 F_p(m)=\sum_{\substack{j\geq1\\mp^j\geq K}}p^{-j},\qquad
 D=\prod_{q<p}q^{E_q},\qquad A=\frac1{p-1}.
\]
Distinctness gives at most one residue for each modulus $mp^j$.
A fibre union bound, followed by Lemma~\ref{lem:product} with
$\phi(x)=(x-\delta)_+$, therefore gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:full}
 \E\pos{\alpha_i-\delta}\leq
 \E\pos{S_p(D)-\delta},\qquad
 S_p(D)=\sum_{m\mid D}F_p(m).
\end{equation}
In applying the lemma, the residues for different $j$ are allowed to differ;
they are separate cylinder terms with coefficients $p^{-j}$. Infinite
exponents are an increasing extension of finite positive sums. Expectations
are finite since $\E\tau(D)=C_{i-1}<\infty$.

Let $s_j=\lceil K/p^j\rceil$, for the finitely many $j$ with $p^j<K$.
Then exactly
\begin{equation}\label{eq:subtract}
 S_p(D)=A\tau(D)-\sum_{j:p^j<K}p^{-j}
                    \#\{m\mid D:m<s_j\}.
\end{equation}
We will subtract only divisors supported on $2$ and $3$. This weakens the
bound in a known direction; it does not assume that all forbidden divisors
are of this form.

All parameters at $2$ and $3$ are zero. Write
$D_s=2^a3^b$ and
$Z=\prod_{5\leq q<p}(E_q+1)$, so $\tau(D)=(a+1)(b+1)Z$.
These variables are independent in the comparison model, and
\[
 \rho_{ab}:=\Pp(E_2=a,E_3=b)
     =\frac{2}{2^{a+1}3^{b+1}},\qquad
 \tau_{ab}=(a+1)(b+1),\qquad \E Z=C_{i-1}/3.
\]
For each state define
\[
 B_{ab}(p)=(p-1)\sum_{j:p^j<K}p^{-j}
       \#\{2^u3^v<s_j:0\leq u\leq a,\ 0\leq v\leq b\}.
\]
Equation \eqref{eq:subtract} implies
\[
 S_p(D)\leq A\{\tau_{ab}Z-B_{ab}(p)\}.
\]
The expression on the right is nonnegative: the subtracted sum is at most
$A\tau_{ab}$ and $Z\geq1$.

\begin{proposition}[Conditional loss bound]\label{prop:conditional}
Retain states $0\leq a\leq A_2$, $0\leq b\leq A_3$, and put
\[
 \eta=3-\sum_{a\leq A_2,b\leq A_3}\rho_{ab}\tau_{ab},\qquad
 t_{ab}=\frac{(p-1)\delta+B_{ab}(p)}{\tau_{ab}}.
\]
For $0\leq\delta<1$, a valid upper bound for the stage loss is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:conditional}
 \frac{\displaystyle
  \sum_{a\leq A_2,b\leq A_3}\rho_{ab}\tau_{ab}
                \E\pos{Z-t_{ab}}+\eta\E Z}
 {(p-1)(1-\delta)}.
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
On each retained state, apply the preceding pointwise bound inside
\eqref{eq:full}. On the omitted states, use
$(S_p(D)-\delta)_+\leq S_p(D)\leq A\tau(D)$.
The expectation of $\tau(D_s)$ is $2\cdot(3/2)=3$, so independence gives
the omitted contribution $A\eta\E Z$. Divide by $1-\delta$ using
\eqref{eq:loss}.
\end{proof}

This proof conditions on the independent comparison variables, not on the
original covering system's valuations. No independence of the distorted
original measure is asserted.

\subsection{Conditioning on more primes}
The same argument applies to any initial set $\mathcal S$ of primes whose
parameters are zero. Write $D_s=\prod_{q\in\mathcal S}q^{E_q}$,
$Z=\prod_{q<p,\,q\notin\mathcal S}(E_q+1)$ and
$c_s=\prod_{q\in\mathcal S}q/(q-1)$. For an exponent vector $e$ put
\[
 \rho_e=\prod_{q\in\mathcal S}\frac{q-1}{q^{e_q+1}},\quad
 \tau_e=\prod_{q\in\mathcal S}(e_q+1),\quad
 B_e=(p-1)\sum_{j:p^j<K}p^{-j}\#\{m\mid D_s:m<s_j\}.
\]
For any finite retained set $G$ of vectors, replace $3$ by $c_s$,
$\eta$ by $c_s-\sum_G\rho_e\tau_e$, and $\E Z$ by $C_{i-1}/c_s$
in Proposition~\ref{prop:conditional}. Its proof is unchanged.
States with identical $\tau_e$ and divisor-count vectors may be grouped:
their coefficients are positive and their excess thresholds are identical.
The integer implementation sums upper bounds for these coefficients, and
uses lower bounds when computing the omitted weighted mean.

\section{A finite upper bound for the excess expectation}
For any nonnegative integrable random variable $Z$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:identity}
 \E\pos{Z-t}=\E Z-t+\E\pos{t-Z}.
\end{equation}
Thus the mean is retained exactly through its Euler product, while only the
bounded lower part of the distribution must be computed. This is the source
of the large gain over bounding the excess by a quadratic moment.

The earlier verifier constructs integer bin representatives $b_0=0,b_1=1$, followed
by
\[
 b_{r+1}=\min\{100000,\ b_r+\max(1,\lfloor b_r/256\rfloor)\}.
\]
Starting at $1$, whenever a multiplier $h=E_q+1$ is applied, its product
with the current representative is rounded \emph{down} to the largest
representative not exceeding it. Hence the retained representative
$\widetilde Z$ is at most the true product $Z$, path by path. A product of
representatives exceeding $100000$ is dropped. Its true product already
exceeds $100000$ and can never return below that cutoff; such a path
contributes zero to $(t-Z)_+$ when $t\leq100000$.

For each prime $q$, choose the least positive integer $H_q$ with
$q^{H_q}\geq2\cdot10^{32}$. Include multipliers $h=1,2,\ldots,H_q$.
The omitted probability at that prime is
\[
 \Pp(E_q\geq H_q)=\nu_q/q^{H_q}.
\]
The probability of ever omitting such a multiplier is at most the sum
$\epsilon$ of these quantities. This union bound also covers paths already
discarded, so it is safely conservative.

Let $U_r$ be upper bounds on the probabilities of the retained rounded
states. For $0\leq t\leq100000$, the coupling gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:excess}
 \E\pos{Z-t}\leq
 \E Z-t+\sum_{b_r<t}(t-b_r)U_r+t\epsilon.
\end{equation}
If the original threshold exceeds $100000$, replace it by $100000$.
This also increases the excess expectation. The verifier first rounds each
threshold downward to a fixed-point rational and then applies this cap.
Monotonicity of $\E(Z-t)_+$ justifies both operations.

The direction of binning is essential: $Z$ is rounded downward only in the
complementary term $\E(t-Z)_+$. The mean $\E Z$ in \eqref{eq:excess}
is the independent exact Euler-product mean, not the mean of the rounded
distribution. This avoids an unaccounted moment error.

\section{First-moment information inside the bins}\label{sec:binmean}
The complementary bound \eqref{eq:excess} can be sharpened without using
smaller bins. Let the cap be $H$ and the bin resolution be an integer
$R\geq2$, with increments $\max(1,\lfloor b/R\rfloor)$. On a retained
path let $z$ be the true product and $b$ its rounded representative.
Every nontrivial multiplication increases $b$ by at least
$g=2R/(R+1)>1$: the downward rounding loses a factor less than $1+1/R$,
and the multiplier is at least $2$. Let $N$ be the largest integer with
$g^N\leq H$. There are at most $N$ nontrivial updates on a retained path,
and therefore
\[
 b\leq z\leq u_b:=\left\lceil b(1+1/R)^N\right\rceil.
\]
For $b<R$ one may set $u_b=b$, since all preceding integer products were
represented exactly. These support bounds are computed with integer powers.

Let $P_b$ denote the mass of retained paths in bin $b$, and
$M_b=\E[Z\mathbf1_{\{\text{retained bin }b\}}]$ their \emph{true}
weighted first moment. Maintain an upper bound $P_b^+$ and a lower bound
$M_b^-$. Under a multiplier $h$ of probability $w_h$, the contribution
to the next mass is $w_hP_b$ and to the next first moment is $hw_hM_b$.
Rounding these respectively upward and downward gives rigorous bounds.

Convexity of $z\mapsto(t-z)_+$ on $[b,u_b]$ gives the following upper
contribution of the bin:
\[
 \begin{cases}
 0,&t\leq b,\\
 \displaystyle\frac{t-b}{u_b-b}(u_bP_b^+-M_b^-),&b<t<u_b,\\
 tP_b^+-M_b^-,&t\geq u_b.
 \end{cases}
\]
The middle line is the chord between the two endpoints. The last line is
exact before rounding, so rounding errors in the representative do not
accumulate as a false mean deficit for bins entirely below the threshold.
Adding $t\epsilon$ for omitted prime-power paths and then using
\eqref{eq:identity} proves an excess upper bound. Discarded paths above
the cap still have zero complementary contribution for $t\leq H$.
Thresholds above the cap are decreased to the cap as before.

For efficient evaluation, the code accumulates prefix sums of $P_b^+$,
$M_b^-$, and of the upward-rounded coefficients
$(u_bP_b^+-M_b^-)/(u_b-b)$ and their products with $b$.
The two latter sums use the \emph{same} rounded coefficient. Thus their
difference is a sum of nonnegative multiples of upper bounds; no subtraction
of an independently rounded upper bound is mistaken for an upper bound.

\subsection{Sharper supports from the rounded-product graph}\label{sec:reachable}
The uniform factor $(1+1/R)^N$ can be replaced by an integer reachability
calculation. Every retained multiplier satisfies $h\leq108$, since
$2^{108}\geq2\cdot10^{32}$ and $q\geq2$. Form a directed graph on the
positive bin representatives. For $2\leq h\leq108$ and $bh\leq H$, put an
edge from $b$ to $r(bh)$, labelled $h$, where $r$ rounds down to a bin.
Every edge strictly increases its representative, so the graph is acyclic.

Set $U_1=1$, set the other values initially to zero, and process vertices
in increasing order. For every outgoing edge from a reachable vertex, set
\[
 U_{r(bh)}\leftarrow\max\{U_{r(bh)},hU_b\}.
\]
The value $U_b$ is the maximum product of edge labels on any graph path
from $1$ to $b$. Every retained probabilistic path is a graph path after
its multipliers equal to $1$ have been omitted. Induction over its edges
therefore gives $Z\leq U_b$. Allowing arbitrary repetitions and orders of
the labels enlarges the set of paths and is harmless for this upper bound.
For prefix-sum evaluation use the monotone endpoints
\[
 u_b=\max\{b,\max_{c\leq b}U_c\}.
\]
They contain every true retained value in their bin. Consequently all
chord and first-moment inequalities of Section~\ref{sec:binmean} remain
valid with these endpoints. The calculation uses exact integers in
\texttt{code/reachable\_support.py}; the verifier also checks $H_q\leq108$
at every update. This improves the moment enclosure without altering the
probability distribution, its mean, or the omitted-path estimates.

\section{Finite conditioning with an unrestricted fallback}\label{sec:sparse}
The omitted-state term $\eta\E Z$ can be avoided. Fix an initial prime
set $\mathcal S$ with zero distortions, and let $G$ be any finite set of
$\mathcal S$-smooth positive integers. Put
\[
 \rho_n=\Pp(D_s=n)=\frac1n\prod_{q\in\mathcal S}\frac{q-1}{q},\quad
 B_n=(p-1)\sum_{j:p^j<K}p^{-j}\#\{m\mid n:m<s_j\},\quad A=(p-1)\delta.
\]
\begin{proposition}[Bounded conditioning correction]\label{prop:sparse}
Let $Y=\tau(D_s)Z$ be the complete independent product. A valid stage-loss
upper bound is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sparse}
 \frac{\displaystyle \E(Y-A)_+
 -\sum_{n\in G}\rho_n\E\min\{B_n,(\tau(n)Z-A)_+\}}
 {(p-1)(1-\delta)}.
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
For a retained state $n$, the divisor subtraction gives
$(p-1)S_p(D)\leq\tau(n)Z-B_n$. Use the identity
\[
 (x-A)_+-(x-A-B)_+=\min\{B,(x-A)_+\}\qquad(B\geq0).
\]
On all states outside $G$, simply use $(p-1)S_p(D)\leq Y$.
Taking expectations and applying \eqref{eq:full} and \eqref{eq:loss}
proves \eqref{eq:sparse}. Independence is used only in the comparison model.
\end{proof}

We choose $G=\{n<L:n\text{ is }\mathcal S\text{-smooth}\}$.
There is no unbounded tail approximation: outside this finite set the
unrestricted excess estimate remains in force. Unlike a discarded first
moment, each omitted correction is bounded by $B_n$.

The first term in \eqref{eq:sparse} is bounded upward by the method of
Section~\ref{sec:binmean}, using the full prime product. To bound a correction
downward, propagate \emph{lower} probability masses $V_b$ for downward bins
of $Z$. Since
$z\mapsto\min\{B_n,(\tau(n)z-A)_+\}$ is nonnegative and increasing,
\[
 \E\min\{B_n,(\tau(n)Z-A)_+\}
 \geq\sum_b V_b\min\{B_n,(\tau(n)b-A)_+\}.
\]
All omitted paths, including prime-power truncations and products above the
cap, contribute nonnegative quantities and may be dropped in this lower
bound. No omitted-probability subtraction is necessary here.
Multiply by lower bounds for $\rho_n$, sum with downward rounding, subtract
from the excess upper bound, and divide upward by $(p-1)(1-\delta)$.
The sign of every rounding operation is therefore fixed explicitly.
Grouping states with the same divisor-count vector and $\tau(n)$ again
preserves the bound, now by summing \emph{lower} probabilities.

\subsection{Nonzero distortion inside the conditioned set}\label{sec:dynamic}
The zero-distortion restriction on $\mathcal S$ is unnecessary. At stage
$p$, use the active set $\mathcal S_p=\{q\in\mathcal S:q<p\}$ and retain
$G_p=\{n<N:n\text{ is }\mathcal S_p\text{-smooth}\}$. For
$\nu_q<q$, the exact comparison probability is
\[
 \rho_{n,p}=\frac1n\prod_{q\in\mathcal S_p}\left(1-\frac{\nu_q}{q}\right)
       \prod_{q\mid n}\frac{\nu_q(q-1)}{q-\nu_q}.
\]
This follows directly by multiplying the atom probabilities of the
independent comparison exponents. Proposition~\ref{prop:sparse} applies
with these weights and the active set; the proof is unchanged.
Unexposed primes never contribute to $D_s$ or to its divisor count.

The dynamic integer implementation tracks lower and upper bounds for the
common normalizer and for each state's coefficient. When $q$ is exposed,
the normalizer is multiplied by $1-\nu_q/q$, and coefficients of states
divisible by $q$ are multiplied by $\nu_q(q-1)/(q-\nu_q)$. States involving
any unexposed prime are excluded. These are rational, positive updates;
the verifier checks their denominators and rounds every product outward.
The remaining-prime product contains only primes outside $\mathcal S$.

\subsection{Joint conditioning on larger-prime presence}\label{sec:presence}
Let $q_0$ be the first prime outside $\mathcal S$, and suppose $p\geq q_0$,
$K\leq pq_0^2$, and $K\leq p^2q_0$. Every forbidden divisor $m$ with
$mp^j<K$ then contains at most one prime outside $\mathcal S$, with
exponent one; such a prime can occur only when $j=1$.
For a retained small-prime state $n$, write
\[
 I_q=\mathbf1_{\{E_q\geq1\}},\quad
 c_q(n)=\#\{d\mid n:dpq<K\},\quad
 a=\sum_{q_0\leq q<p}I_q,\quad J=\sum_{q_0\leq q<p}c_q(n)I_q.
\]
The $I_q$ are independent Bernoulli variables with probabilities
$\nu_q/q$ in the comparison model. The full forbidden-divisor subtraction
is exactly $B_n+(p-1)J/p$, while
$Y\geq\tau(n)2^a$. Thus an additional valid lower bound for the correction
subtracted from $\E(Y-A)_+$ is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:presence}
 \sum_{n\in G_p}\rho_{n,p}\,
 \E\min\left\{B_n+\frac{p-1}{p}J,
       \big(\tau(n)2^{\min(a,12)}-A\big)_+\right\}.
\end{equation}
The identity in Proposition~\ref{prop:sparse}, now with the full
forbidden-divisor count, proves the claim. Capping $a$ at $12$ decreases
the integrand, and discards no probability. The old correction and
\eqref{eq:presence} each lower-bound the same full correction, so their
maximum is still a valid lower bound.

For each divisor-count pattern, a finite recurrence tracks the pair
$(\min(a,12),J)$. At prime $q$ it either stays in place, with probability
$1-\nu_q/q$, or moves to $(\min(a+1,12),J+c_q)$, with probability
$\nu_q/q$. The code rounds both contributions downward and then evaluates
the nonnegative integrand with exact rational arithmetic. The required
inequalities on $p,q_0,K$ are checked before every use. This calculation
retains the dependence between product size and forbidden divisors;
it never assumes those two quantities are independent.

The maximum can also be taken \emph{before} summing over conditioned states.
For each $n$, both conditional expressions lower-bound the same conditional
correction. Therefore summing their pointwise maximum with weights
$\rho_{n,p}$ is valid and at least as strong as taking the maximum of the
two sums. The option \texttt{statewise\_presence} implements this refinement.
Its integer evaluation groups states by all divisor counts needed by either
expression, rounds both conditional expectations down, and then takes their
maximum. No statistical independence between the two estimates is needed.

\subsection{Conditioning on a decreasing event}
For the specific retained set $G=\{D_s<L\}$, the correction can be
strengthened further. For each fixed $z\geq1$ the function
\[
 g_z(e)=\min\{B_e,(\tau(e)z-A)_+\}
\]
is increasing in each exponent: both $B_e$ and $\tau(e)$ are increasing.
The event $G$ is decreasing. Independent coordinates have nonnegative
covariance for increasing functions, so
\[
 \E[g_z\mathbf1_G]\leq\E[g_z]\Pp(G),\qquad
 \E[g_z]\geq\frac{\E[g_z\mathbf1_G]}{\Pp(G)}.
\]
For completeness, the covariance assertion follows in one coordinate from
$2\operatorname{Cov}(f(X),h(X))=
\E[(f(X)-f(X'))(h(X)-h(X'))]\geq0$, with $X'$ an independent copy.
Induction over independent coordinates proves the assertion in a finite
product; truncation and bounded convergence handle the geometric tails.
Apply it to $g_z$ and $1-\mathbf1_G$. Here $g_z$ is bounded because
$B_e$ counts only divisors below finitely many fixed thresholds.

Integrating over the independent remaining-prime product $Z$ proves that
the correction in \eqref{eq:sparse} may be divided by $\Pp(G)$.
The integer implementation divides the already certified lower correction
by an \emph{upper} bound for $\Pp(G)$ and rounds downward. It uses this
option only for the stated decreasing smooth-number cutoff, not for an
arbitrary finite retained set. This is an association inequality within
the independent comparison model, with no assertion of independence or
association for the original distorted covering measure.

\subsection{Joint product and forbidden-divisor conditioning}\label{sec:jointproduct}
The presence-only correction can be strengthened by tracking the full
product of the remaining comparison factors. Suppose the first remaining
prime is $q_0$, $K\leq pq_0^2$, and $p^2q_0\geq K$.
For a fixed small-prime state $n$, every forbidden divisor contains at most
one remaining prime, to exponent one. Thus its full forbidden contribution is
\[
 B=B_0(n)+\frac{p-1}{p}J,\qquad
 J=\sum_{q_0\leq q<p}c_q(n)\mathbf1_{E_q\geq1},\quad
 c_q(n)=\#\{d\mid n:dpq<K\}.
\]
Put $Z=\prod_{q_0\leq q<p}(E_q+1)$, $\tau=\tau(n)$, and
$A=(p-1)\delta$. The exact conditional correction is
\[
 \E\min\{B,(\tau Z-A)_+\}
 =\E B-\E\min\{B,(A+B-\tau Z)_+\}.
\]
Its mean is explicit:
$\E B=B_0+(p-1)p^{-1}\sum_q c_q\nu_q/q$.
Let $h$ be the number of remaining primes with $pq<K$. Since
$B_0\leq\tau$ and $c_q\leq\tau$, we have $B\leq\tau(1+h)$.
Consequently the complementary integrand is zero for
$Z>H=\lceil A\rceil+1+h$.

The joint law of $(Z,J)$ for $Z\leq H$ is computed by a finite recurrence.
At prime $q$, exponent zero multiplies a state probability by $1-\nu_q/q$;
exponent $a\geq1$ sends $(z,j)$ to $(z(a+1),j+c_q)$ with multiplier
$\nu_q(q-1)/q^{a+1}$. Retain exactly the transitions with $z(a+1)\leq H$.
The product never decreases, so no discarded path can return. This gives
the entire bounded complement, with no assumption on omitted exponents.

The verifier rounds the mean of $B$ downward, all retained probabilities
and complementary summands upward, and clips the resulting correction
below at zero. It then weights this lower correction by each retained
small-state probability rounded downward. Outside the retained small states
the correction is still omitted, as before. This is an alternative to
enumerating all early comparison integers: it conditions on the exact
remaining product and its forbidden-divisor contribution jointly.
The implementation is \texttt{code/joint\_product\_bound.py}, activated
by the witness flag \texttt{joint\_product}.

This also permits a different split of the comparison coordinates.
With only the first eight primes explicitly enumerated, $q_0=23$;
for the cutoffs tested here the structural hypotheses hold from $p=29$.
The earlier stages are covered by exact early evaluation. The joint-product
correction then retains the forbidden divisors involving primes $q\geq23$,
while the retained set of small-prime states has a smaller omitted tail.
In this variant the numerical prefix is extended through $150$ primes,
so that $23p_{150}>K$ and the later adjoint again has no joint-product
corrections left to differentiate. The verifier checks the structural
hypotheses before using this correction; it never extrapolates the
one-remaining-prime identity to stages where two such primes can occur.

There is an exact monotonicity principle behind this change. Let
$D_{\mathcal S}$ be the part of the comparison integer supported on
$\mathcal S$, and let $g(D)$ be the full nonnegative forbidden-divisor
correction. If $\mathcal S\subset\mathcal T$, then
$D_{\mathcal S}\leq D_{\mathcal T}$ pointwise, and therefore
\[
 \E\bigl[g(D)\mathbf1_{D_{\mathcal S}<N}\bigr]
 \geq \E\bigl[g(D)\mathbf1_{D_{\mathcal T}<N}\bigr].
\]
At stages where the structural hypotheses hold for both splits, the exact
joint-product recurrence computes these same retained corrections.
Thus reducing the explicitly enumerated set can strengthen the estimate:
more full comparison states are retained, while the remaining coordinates
are still integrated jointly. The computational cost shifts to the joint
product distribution.

\section{Exact finite evaluation of early lower tails}\label{sec:earlyexact}
There is another way to retain the minimum-modulus restriction, which does
not discard large comparison integers. At stage $p$, include \emph{all}
primes $q<p$ in $D$, put $L_0=\lceil K/p\rceil$, $A=(p-1)\delta$, and write
\[
 B(D)=(p-1)\sum_{j:p^j<K}p^{-j}\#\{d\mid D:dp^j<K\},\qquad
 W(D)=\tau(D)-B(D)=(p-1)S_p(D).
\]
If $D\geq L_0^2$, pairing divisors $d$ and $D/d$ shows that at most half
the divisors lie below $L_0$, and hence below any of the smaller thresholds.
Consequently $B(D)\leq\tau(D)/2$ and $W(D)\geq\tau(D)/2$.
It follows that the positive part $(A-W(D))_+$ is supported on the finite set
\begin{equation}\label{eq:earlyfinite}
 \{D<L_0^2\}\ \cup\ \{\tau(D)\leq\lfloor2A\rfloor\}.
\end{equation}
The second set is finite because the number of primes is finite and every
exponent is at most $\lfloor2A\rfloor-1$. When the floor is zero that set
is empty. The first set is an ordinary finite smooth-number enumeration.

Let $M_1=\E S_p(D)$, evaluated by the restricted first-moment Euler product
and finite divisor sum already used above. The identity for a positive part
gives the exact comparison expectation
\[
 \E(S_p(D)-\delta)_+
 =M_1-\delta+\frac1{p-1}\E(A-W(D))_+.
\]
Thus the stage loss is bounded by this expression divided by $1-\delta$.
The last expectation is an \emph{exact finite sum} over
\eqref{eq:earlyfinite}; there is no omitted-exponent probability in this step.
The two enumerations are made disjoint by excluding $D<L_0^2$ from the
divisor-count enumeration. The probability of each state is the product
of the comparison probabilities in \eqref{eq:exponent}.

\texttt{code/early\_exact\_states.py} performs the integer enumeration and
uses upper bounds for every nonnegative summand. The first moment is rounded
up, the subtraction of $\delta$ uses an outward interval, and division is
outward. All numerators defining $W$ are integers over a common power of $p$.
This alternative is practical at early primes, where $2A$ and the number of
prime coordinates are small. The witness specifies the last prime at which
it is used; at every such stage the verifier takes the minimum with the
other valid loss bounds.

\subsection{Conditioning the complement, including omitted configurations}
\label{sec:conditionedcomplement}
The full-product calculation can also be applied directly to the preceding
complement identity. This avoids subtracting a retained correction from an
unrestricted, binned baseline. Let $D=nD_R$, where $n$ is supported on the
explicitly conditioned primes and $Z=\tau(D_R)$. Retain $n<N$, assume
$N\geq L_0^2$, and impose the structural hypotheses of
Section~\ref{sec:jointproduct}. For a retained $n$, the conditional
complement is
\[
 F_n=\E\left(A+B_0(n)+\frac{p-1}{p}J-\tau(n)Z\right)_+.
\]
Its integrand vanishes for $Z>\lceil A\rceil+1+h$, by exactly the same
bound $B\leq\tau(n)(1+h)$ used there. Thus the finite joint recurrence
computes $F_n$ without an exponent truncation error.

On the omitted event $n\geq N$, the full comparison integer satisfies
$D\geq L_0^2$. Divisor pairing therefore gives $W(D)\geq\tau(n)Z/2$.
Write
\[
 a_t=\Pp(\tau(n)=t),\quad
 b_t=\Pp(\tau(n)=t,\ n<N),\quad r_z=\Pp(Z=z).
\]
Independence of the two prime sets proves the rigorous bound
\begin{equation}\label{eq:conditionedcomplement}
 \E(A-W(D))_+
 \leq \sum_{n<N}\Pp(n)F_n
 +\sum_{tz\leq\lfloor2A\rfloor}(a_t-b_t)r_z
                       (A-tz/2)_+.
\end{equation}
The second sum is finite. The laws $a_t$ and $r_z$ through
$\lfloor2A\rfloor$ are obtained by the same product recurrence, with all
additive coefficients zero. No omitted configuration is assumed to have
zero mass. Multiplicative transitions cannot return below the cutoff.

There is also a monotonicity principle for this bound before rounding.
For a smaller conditioned prime set, the event $n<N$ becomes larger.
On the configurations newly retained, the exact complement replaces the
pairing upper bound $(A-\tau(D)/2)_+$. Thus, wherever the structural
hypotheses hold for both splits, reducing the conditioned prime set cannot
increase the right side of \eqref{eq:conditionedcomplement}. This compares
exact evaluations of the stated bound; it does not compare different
rounding settings or different choices of the early distortion parameters.

The implementation \texttt{code/conditioned\_complement\_bound.py}
rounds retained configuration probabilities, $F_n$, $a_t$, and $r_z$
upward, and $b_t$ downward. It replaces $a_t-b_t$ by the nonnegative
upper bound $\max(0,a_t^{\rm up}-b_t^{\rm lo})$. All summands are
nonnegative, so these operations preserve an upper bound. Substitution
in $(M_1-\delta+\E(A-W)_+/(p-1))/(1-\delta)$ gives a certified stage
loss. The verifier takes the minimum with the other valid bounds.
The witness field \texttt{conditioned\_complement\_prime} specifies the
last stage at which this additional bound is used. Independent rational
checks enumerate the complete finite complement support in eighteen
small cases, including nonzero distortions and omitted configurations.

\section{An explicit third-moment stopping criterion}\label{sec:thirdtail}
There is a useful alternative to the second-moment termination theorem.
The comparison argument also applies to the increasing convex function
$x^3$. For the complete product $Y=\prod_{q\leq P}(E_q+1)$, put
\[
 U_k=\E Y^3=\prod_{q\leq P}
 \left(1+\nu_q\frac{7q^2-2q+1}{(q-1)^3}\right),\qquad P=p_k.
\]
The Euler factor follows by summing
$1+\nu_q\sum_{a\geq1}((a+1)^3-a^3)q^{-a}$.
Choose a constant future distortion $d\in(0,1/2]$ and
$\nu=(1-d)^{-1}$. The elementary inequality
\[
 (x-d)_+\leq\frac{4x^3}{27d^2}\quad(x\geq0)
\]
follows by maximizing $(x-d)/x^3$, whose maximum is attained at
$x=3d/2$. Thus the loss at a later prime $p$ is at most
\[
 \frac{4}{27d^2(1-d)}\frac{U(p^-)}{(p-1)^3}.
\]

For an explicit infinite sum we use Dusart,
\emph{Estimates of Some Functions Over Primes without R.H.},
\href{https://arxiv.org/pdf/1002.0442}{arXiv:1002.0442v1},
Theorem 6.9, inequality (6.6):
$\pi(x)\leq x/(\log x-1.1)$ for $x\geq60184$.
Assume $P\geq60184$, put $l=\log P$, and choose a rational
$c\geq l/(l-1.1)$. Then $\pi(x)\leq cx/\log x$ for all $x\geq P$.
Partial summation, using the exact count $\pi(P)=k$, gives
\[
 \sum_{P<q\leq x}\frac1q
 \leq \frac{c}{l}-\frac{k}{P}
       +c\log\frac{\log x}{l}.
\]
Set
\[
 r=\frac{P}{P-1},\quad \alpha=7\nu r^3,\quad
 m=\lceil\alpha c\rceil,\quad
 z=\alpha\left(\frac{c}{l}-\frac{k}{P}\right).
\]
The third-moment Euler increment at $q>P$ is at most $\alpha/q$.
Since $\log(1+t)\leq t$, the preceding prime sum proves
\[
 U(p^-)\leq U_k e^z(\log p/l)^m.
\]
Suppose $0\leq z<1$ and $m<3l$, as the verifier checks. The function
$f(x)=(\log x/l)^m/x^3$ is decreasing on $[P,\infty)$.
Another partial summation yields
\[
 \sum_{p>P}f(p)
 \leq c\left(\frac{3J_{m-1}}{l}
             -\frac{mJ_{m-2}}{l^2}\right)-\frac{k}{P^3},
\]
where integration by parts evaluates the integrals exactly as
\[
 J_j=\int_P^\infty\frac{(\log x/l)^j}{x^3}\,dx
 =\frac1{2P^2}\sum_{h=0}^j\frac{j!}{(j-h)!(2l)^h}.
\]
The boundary term $-k/P^3$ is retained; omitting it would significantly
weaken the estimate. Using $(p-1)^{-3}\leq r^3p^{-3}$ and
$e^z\leq(1-z)^{-1}$ gives the entirely explicit tail bound
\begin{equation}\label{eq:thirdtail}
 \mathcal T_k=
 \frac{4U_kr^3}{27d^2(1-d)(1-z)}
 \left[c\left(\frac{3J_{m-1}}l-
                   \frac{mJ_{m-2}}{l^2}\right)-\frac{k}{P^3}\right].
\end{equation}
If $\mu_k>\mathcal T_k$, the sum of all stage losses is less than one.
Prefix preservation and the union bound, as in equation (13) in BBMST's
proof of Theorem 3.1, imply noncoverage for every finite covering candidate.
This argument closes the entire infinite prime tail; it does not infer
noncoverage merely from survival through a finite list of primes.

\texttt{code/third\_moment\_tail.py} evaluates \eqref{eq:thirdtail}
using the existing outward integer intervals. It encloses $\log P$ by
the rational atanh series, rounds $c$ upward to a multiple of $10^{-6}$,
and verifies every stated hypothesis. No floating-point logarithm or
exponential participates in this certificate.

\subsection{Interpolation of the tail growth exponent}
The upward rounding of $\alpha c$ to an integer can be sharpened.
Let $s$ be a rational upper bound for $\alpha c$, let $m=\lceil s\rceil$,
and put $\theta=s-(m-1)$. For $t\geq1$, convexity in the exponent gives
\[
 t^{\alpha c}\leq t^s\leq(1-\theta)t^{m-1}+\theta t^m.
\]
Apply the already proved prime-sum bound separately to these two integer
powers and combine them with the nonnegative weights. This replaces the
bracket in \eqref{eq:thirdtail} by the corresponding weighted combination
of the brackets for $m-1$ and $m$. The verifier checks the decreasing-function
condition for the larger exponent and evaluates $\theta$ exactly at its
fixed-point rational value. This option avoids any unevaluated fractional
power or numerical integration in the proof.

\subsection{Using the shifted logarithm directly}
One can use Dusart's bound without replacing its denominator by a constant
multiple of $\log x$. Put $\ell=\log P-1.1$. Partial summation gives
\[
 \sum_{P<q\leq x}\frac1q
 \leq\frac1\ell-\frac{k}{P}
       +\log\frac{\log x-1.1}{\ell}.
\]
Indeed the endpoint term is at most $1/\ell$ and the integral is the stated
logarithmic ratio. The preceding third-moment proof now applies with
$c=1$, $l=\ell$, and growth exponent $\alpha$, using
$(\log x-1.1)/\ell$ throughout. The integration-by-parts recurrence for
$J_j$ is unchanged. The option \texttt{shifted\_tail} implements exactly
this variant, including the same monotonicity and positivity checks.

\subsection{Fractional-moment termination}\label{sec:fractional}
The moment order need not be an integer. For $1<s\leq3$, set
\[
 U_k^{(s)}=\E Y^s=
 \prod_{q\leq P}\left(1+\nu_q a_s(q)\right),\qquad
 a_s(q)=\sum_{a\geq1}\frac{(a+1)^s-a^s}{q^a}.
\]
Maximizing $(x-d)/x^s$ gives, for $x\geq0$,
\[
 (x-d)_+\leq\frac{(s-1)^{s-1}}{s^s d^{s-1}}x^s.
\]
The same convex comparison therefore bounds the later stage loss by
$\kappa_s(d)U^{(s)}(p^-)/(p-1)^s$, where
$\kappa_s(d)=(s-1)^{s-1}/[s^s d^{s-1}(1-d)]$.

For integers $a\geq1$,
\[
 (a+1)^s-a^s\leq(2^s-1)\frac{a(a+1)}2.
\]
To see this, write the left side as
$s\int_a^{a+1}t^{s-1}\,dt$. Its ratio to $a(a+1)$ is $s$ times
the $t^2$-weighted average of $t^{s-3}$ on that interval, multiplied by
$1+1/[3a(a+1)]$. Both factors are at most their values at $a=1$:
the first because $s\leq3$ and the integer intervals are ordered, and the
second directly. Summing the displayed bound gives
\[
 a_s(q)\leq\frac{2^s-1}{q}\left(\frac q{q-1}\right)^3.
\]
For $r=P/(P-1)$, $\nu=(1-d)^{-1}$, $\ell=\log P-1.1$, and
$\alpha=\nu(2^s-1)r^3$, the shifted prime sum thus bounds moment growth
by $U_k^{(s)}e^z[(\log x-1.1)/\ell]^\alpha$, where
$z=\alpha(1/\ell-k/P)$.

Choose a rational upper bound $u\geq\max(\alpha,2)$, put
$m=\max(3,\lceil u\rceil)$ and
$\theta=u-(m-1)$. Assume $0\leq z<1$ and $m<s\ell$.
With $\beta=s-1$, define
\[
 J_j^{(s)}=\frac1{\beta P^\beta}
       \sum_{h=0}^j\frac{j!}{(j-h)!(\beta\ell)^h},\qquad
 Q_j^{(s)}=\frac{sJ_{j-1}^{(s)}}\ell
       -\frac{jJ_{j-2}^{(s)}}{\ell^2}-\frac{k}{P^s}.
\]
These follow by integrating $x^{-s}[(\log x-1.1)/\ell]^j$ and then
applying partial summation to the decreasing function
$x^{-s}[(\log x-1.1)/\ell]^j$. Convex interpolation of the exponent gives
the explicit infinite loss bound
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fractionaltail}
 \mathcal T_k^{(s)}=
 \frac{\kappa_s(d)U_k^{(s)}r^s}{1-z}
 \left[(1-\theta)Q_{m-1}^{(s)}+\theta Q_m^{(s)}\right].
\end{equation}
Positivity of $\mu_k-\mathcal T_k^{(s)}$ proves noncoverage by the same
prefix-preservation and union-bound argument.

The implemented orders are dyadic rationals $s=a/b$ with $1<s\leq3$
and $b\in\{4,8,16,32\}$. An omitted denominator in an older witness means
$b=4$. All powers are evaluated by integer products and outward integer
square-root enclosures, iterated $\log_2 b$ times for the fractional part,
so no floating-point power is a proof input. This permits a finer moment
search, including $43/16$, without changing the tail argument. Each Euler
series is summed through the least $H$ with $q^H\geq10^{35}$.
Its nonnegative remainder is bounded by $2^s-1$ times
\[
 \frac{(H+1)(H+2)}{2q^{H+1}}
 \left(1-\frac{H+3}{q(H+1)}\right)^{-1},
\]
because successive terms of the dominating series have at most that
geometric ratio. The denominator is checked positive. These enclosures,
the logarithm, and every hypothesis of \eqref{eq:fractionaltail} are
evaluated with integer intervals in \texttt{code/fractional\_moment\_tail.py}.

\subsection{Moment orders between three and four}
The same tail integration also applies at $s=4$, using the exact increment
\[
 a_4(q)=\frac{15q^3+5q^2+5q-1}{(q-1)^4}
 \leq\frac{15+5/P+5/P^2}{q}\left(\frac P{P-1}\right)^4
 \quad(q>P).
\]
This identity follows by summing
$\sum_{a\geq1}(4a^3+6a^2+4a+1)q^{-a}$.
Set $\alpha=\nu(15+5/P+5/P^2)r^4$ and use the same formulas for
$z,m,\theta,J_j^{(s)},Q_j^{(s)}$ and $\kappa_s(d)$, checking their
hypotheses anew. This does not extend the earlier $s\leq3$ increment
inequality to $s=4$; it supplies a separate valid growth bound.
More generally, for $3<s\leq4$ the ratio
\[
 \frac{(a+1)^s-a^s}{(a+1)^4-a^4}
 =\frac{s}{4}\frac{\int_a^{a+1}t^3t^{s-4}\,dt}
                         {\int_a^{a+1}t^3\,dt}
 \leq\frac{2^s-1}{15}
\]
decreases over successive integer intervals. Hence the same proof works
with $\alpha=\nu(2^s-1)(15+5/P+5/P^2)r^4/15$.
The Euler sum is still evaluated directly by outward root enclosures.
For its omitted terms use $(2^s-1)/15$ times the fourth increment
$4a^3+6a^2+4a+1$. Starting at $a=H+1$, successive terms have ratio at
most $(H+2)^3/[q(H+1)^3]$, giving an explicit geometric remainder.
The same reasoning extends to $4<s\leq5$. In this range use
\[
 a_5(q)=\frac{31q^4+56q^3+36q^2-4q+1}{(q-1)^5},\qquad
 \alpha=\frac{\nu(2^s-1)}{31}
       (31+56/P+36/P^2+1/P^4)r^5.
\]
Indeed, $((a+1)^s-a^s)/((a+1)^5-a^5)$ is a decreasing
weighted average of $t^{s-5}$ and is at most $(2^s-1)/31$.
The positive coefficients of $5a^4+10a^3+10a^2+5a+1$ give the
Euler-remainder ratio $(H+2)^4/[q(H+1)^4]$.
Every hypothesis of the tail integration must still be checked:
in particular, large moment orders can fail $m<s(\log P-1.1)$ at a
shorter horizon. Such an order is inadmissible there. The integer
verifier rejects an inadmissible listed order rather than ignoring it.
Thus the implemented dyadic orders $1<s\leq5$ have explicit increment
and tail bounds whenever their stated analytic hypotheses hold.

\subsection{Stratifying the infinite tail by the terminal product}
\label{sec:stratifiedtail}
Write $Y=\prod_{q\leq P}(E_q+1)$ for the independent comparison product.
For any event $G$ determined by these earlier comparison coordinates,
future factors remain independent. The preceding tail proof therefore
applies with $U_k^{(s)}$ replaced by $\E[Y^s\mathbf1_G]$. Let $b_s$ be
the certified tail coefficient for initial moment one. For any finite
partition $G_1,\ldots,G_m,G_*$ of the comparison space and finite set
$\mathcal S$ of admissible moment orders,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:stratifiedtail}
 \sum_{i>k}\Pp_i(B_i)
 \leq\sum_{j=1}^m\min_{s\in\mathcal S}
                 b_s\E[Y^s\mathbf1_{G_j}]
       +\min_{s\in\mathcal S}b_s\E[Y^s\mathbf1_{G_*}].
\end{equation}
The split is made \emph{after} the convex comparison with the independent
model. No conditional comparison theorem for indicators under the original
distorted measure is assumed.

Take $G_j$ to be the retained product-bin histories and $G_*$ their entire
complement, including discarded high exponents and products. On $G_j$,
$l_j\leq Y\leq u_j$. Write $P_j=\Pp(G_j)$ and
$M_j=\E[Y\mathbf1_{G_j}]$. Suppose their enclosures are
$P_j^-\leq P_j\leq P_j^+$ and $M_j^-\leq M_j\leq M_j^+$.
For $s>1$, Jensen's inequality and the lower support endpoint give
\[
 L_{s,j}=\max\left\{l_j^{s-1}M_j^-,
             \frac{(M_j^-)^s}{(P_j^+)^{s-1}}\right\}
 \leq\E[Y^s\mathbf1_{G_j}],
\]
where the second term is zero if $P_j^+=0$.
Convex secants give the upper bound
\[
 V_{s,j}=\min\left\{u_j^sP_j^+,\,
 \frac{l_j^s(u_jP_j^+-M_j^-)
       +u_j^s(M_j^+-l_jP_j^-)}{u_j-l_j}\right\}
 \geq\E[Y^s\mathbf1_{G_j}].
\]
For a singleton support use $V_{s,j}=l_j^sP_j^+$.
Thus the omitted part has the moment upper bound
$V_{s,*}=\max(0,(U_k^{(s)})^+-\sum_j L_{s,j})$.
Substituting $V_{s,j},V_{s,*}$ into \eqref{eq:stratifiedtail} proves
the implemented bound, with every power and summand rounded outward.

The existing bin recursion stores upper masses and lower first moments.
The opposite endpoints can be recovered without a second recursion.
At most $109$ operations per bin per prime introduce rounding error,
each less than one integer unit at scale $10^{40}$. Probability
transitions contract total error. First-moment transitions amplify it
by at most $1+\nu_q/(q-1)$. With $B$ bins and $k$ processed primes,
the aggregate probability error is at most $109kB$ integer units and
the aggregate moment error at most
$\lceil109kB C_k^+\rceil$ units. Here $C_k^+$ is expressed in ordinary
units, not fixed-point integers. These aggregate bounds also bound each
individual bin error. The exponent cutoff used by the recursion is at
most $108$ at every prime, which justifies the stated operation bound.
Discarded paths belong to $G_*$; their mass is not counted as rounding
error or set to zero.

\texttt{code/stratified\_moment\_tail.py} implements this argument.
The witness explicitly lists \texttt{stratified\_tail\_orders}.
An optional nonempty subset \texttt{stratified\_overflow\_orders} restricts
the orders used for the last minimum in \eqref{eq:stratifiedtail}.
This remains a valid upper bound and avoids computing unused global
Euler moments; every retained bin still uses the full listed set.
The checker compares the resulting tail with the original single-moment
tail and takes the smaller valid upper bound. Nine exact partition checks
and twelve exact bin-recursion checks test the assembly and rounding
enclosures independently.

\subsection{Refining an initial segment without repeating the long suffix}
Fix the complete rational parameter sequence. The independent comparison
distribution at each stage depends on that sequence, not on which valid
numerical upper bound was chosen for earlier losses. Suppose a completed
base calculation uses stage bounds $b_i$, terminal survivor
$\mu_k=1-\sum_{i\leq k}b_i$, and tail bound $\mathcal T_k$ at cutoff
$K_0$. Recompute its initial segment through $p_h\geq K_0$ exactly,
obtaining $\mu_h^{\rm old}$, and compute valid refined bounds through
the same stage at $K_1\leq K_0$, obtaining $\mu_h^{\rm new}$.
The parameters are identical throughout. After $h$, every modulus newly
allowed by lowering the cutoff has already been exposed. All base suffix
bounds and the tail therefore remain valid. The combined certificate is
\[
 \mu_k^{\rm new}=\mu_k-\mu_h^{\rm old}+\mu_h^{\rm new},\qquad
 \mu_k^{\rm new}>\mathcal T_k.
\]
This subtracts an \emph{identical numerical prefix sum}, not an arbitrary
lower bound for an unknown probability. Binning is only a device for
computing comparison expectations: the suffix retains its independently
computed base bins, rather than restarting those bins from the refined
prefix. Nonnegative stage losses ensure positivity at all intermediate
suffix stages once the final survivor is positive.

\texttt{code/verify\_prefix\_refinement.py} checks exact agreement of the
parameters and base-prefix settings, and allows only the cutoff and bin
resolution to change in the refined prefix. Its default command replays

\section{Parameter search, independent checks, and reproduction}
The exploratory program maintains the comparison distribution in binary64
arithmetic. At each step it numerically minimizes the next upper estimate
of $T_i/\mu_i$ over $0\leq\delta_i\leq1/2$, comparing the conditional
loss with the first- and second-moment alternatives. Golden-section searches
use a fixed number of iterations. The resulting parameters are quantized
to the rational witness. This is systematic local optimization, not a
claim of global optimality of the entire parameter sequence.

For the finite-conditioning search we also screen the policy parameter
$\lambda\in\{0.75,0.9,1,1.1,1.25\}$. At each stage the trial objective is
\[
 \log\left(1+\frac{a_i}{1-\delta}\right)
 -\lambda\log\left(1-\frac{\ell_i(\delta)}{\widehat\mu_{i-1}}\right),
 \qquad a_i=\frac{3p_i-1}{(p_i-1)^2},
\]
where $\ell_i$ is the candidate loss bound. The policy $\lambda=0.9$
gave the best stage-$15000$ result in the fixed $K=20000$ screening run;
the candidates and endpoint comparisons are saved in
\texttt{data/sparse-policy-sweep.json}. This is a reproducible finite
policy search, with no assertion of global optimality.

\subsection{Optimization of the complete parameter sequence}
The new exploratory program \texttt{code/global\_distortion\_search.py}
optimizes a terminal stopping margin, rather than optimizing one stage at
a time. For a fixed terminal index $k$, let
$H_k=k(\log k+\log\log k-3)^2$ and minimize
\[
 J=-\widehat\mu_k+\widehat T_k/H_k.
\]
A negative value is an experimental candidate for certification. Hats
emphasize that this search uses binary64 arithmetic; its objective value
is never accepted as a proof inequality.

Let $x_i$ contain the probability vectors, true bin moments, and Euler
first moment before stage $i$. Its update is linear,
$x_{i+1}=F_i(\delta_i)x_i$, and the stage loss is affine in $x_i$.
With terminal costate zero, reverse accumulation gives
\[
 \lambda_i=\nabla_x\ell_i+F_i^{\mathsf T}\lambda_{i+1},\qquad
 \frac{\partial J}{\partial\delta_i}
 =\frac{\partial\ell_i}{\partial\delta_i}
  +\lambda_{i+1}^{\mathsf T}\frac{\partial F_i}{\partial\delta_i}x_i
  +\frac{\widehat T_k}{H_k}
       \frac{a_i\nu_i^2}{1+a_i\nu_i}.
\]
Here $a_i=(3p_i-1)/(p_i-1)^2$. Thus the derivative includes the effect of
an early parameter on all later distributions and losses. The code
checkpoints the forward calculation and reconstructs short blocks during
the reverse pass to limit memory use.

The parameters are represented by piecewise-linear perturbations in
$\log p_i$ of $\log(2\delta_i/(1-2\delta_i))$, and searched by a
quasi-Newton method with backtracking. The full-horizon runs adjust all
parameters through the stopping index. This is optimization of a global
objective, \emph{not} a proof of a global minimum: the objective is
piecewise differentiable and the numerical search can reach local minima.
Finite-difference checks are separate from the exact certificate checker.

For searches using Section~\ref{sec:thirdtail}, replace the terminal cost
$\widehat T_k/H_k$ by $b_k\widehat U_k$, where $b_k$ is the certified
tail coefficient for $U_k=1$, converted to binary64 only for the search.
The last term in the derivative is then
$b_k\widehat U_k a_i^{(3)}\nu_i^2/(1+a_i^{(3)}\nu_i)$, with
$a_i^{(3)}=(7p_i^2-2p_i+1)/(p_i-1)^3$. The same reverse calculation and
independent derivative checks apply.

For the dynamic and joint-presence runs, the implementation
\texttt{code/frozen\_prefix\_search.py} fixes the first $100$ parameters
and optimizes all subsequent parameters together. At $p_{100}=541$,
every forbidden divisor is already supported on the conditioned primes,
since $59p_{100}>K$ for the cutoffs used here. All conditioned-prime
probabilities are then fixed. Thus the adjoint equations above apply to
the subsequent calculation without omitting derivatives of the early
conditioning weights. No optimization of those fixed early parameters is
claimed. These exploratory runs use $d=493/1000$ with the interpolated
exponent bound; the $14900$ certificate selects $d=1/2$ after a
separate rational comparison of tail parameters. Extending the finite horizon is another search variable:
the code records the survivor and tail estimates at several terminal
indices, and the exact witness can be shortened to any chosen endpoint.

The additional program \texttt{code/joint\_distortion\_search.py} removes
the fixed-prefix restriction. Starting after primes $2,3$, it varies the
fourteen parameters through prime $53$ individually, six linear controls
over the rest of the first $100$ primes, and sixteen logarithmic-prime
controls over all later stages. In those earlier searches the first two
distortions remain zero.
The eight-prime conditioning variant extends the prefix to $150$ primes
and keeps the same fourteen individually controlled early parameters.
The optimization uses a projected quasi-Newton search with backtracking;
the tail objective may be the third or the fractional moment criterion.
The revised search scales the early perturbation at stage $i$ by
$s_i=\min(0.1,\max(0.002,\delta_i^{\rm seed}))$, so that the much smaller
early distortions do not dominate an unscaled search direction. Outward
directions at active box constraints are removed before the descent test.
The saved history includes the projected gradient and individual gradient
components. A centered difference of prefix states is used in the adjoint
pullback to reduce cancellation; a forward finite-difference option is
available for exploratory speed. None of these search derivatives or
convergence diagnostics enters the integer proof. The number of individual
early controls can also be increased by the seed field
\texttt{individual\_prefix\_count}.

The distribution-stratified tail also supplies a directly differentiated
terminal objective. For the search, fix an overflow order $t$ (here $5/4$)
and choose the least secant bound independently in each retained bin.
If its selected order is $s_j$, write the corresponding secant as
$a_jM_j+c_jP_j$. On the Jensen branch of the overflow estimate, the
terminal derivatives are
\[
 \partial_{P_j}F=b_{s_j}c_j+b_t(t-1)(M_j/P_j)^t,\qquad
 \partial_{M_j}F=b_{s_j}a_j-b_t t(M_j/P_j)^{t-1},\qquad
 \partial_{U^{(t)}}F=b_t.
\]
The code handles zero masses, singleton supports and the lower-endpoint
branch separately. These derivatives initialize the reverse calculation
instead of zero terminal bin costates. The Euler-moment derivative uses
the overflow contribution $b_tU^{(t)}$, not the entire stratified tail.
The implementation is \texttt{code/stratified\_tail\_search.py}.
The certificate still evaluates every inequality independently in integers.

The subsequent block search also scales the initial inverse Hessian by
the prime at each logarithmic control knot. Its diagonal entry at knot
$q_j$ is $h_0q_j/q_0$. Later parameters have smaller individual effects,
and this scaling allows larger coordinated updates there. Backtracking
still requires a decrease in the actual terminal objective. This is a
search preconditioner, not a change to a proof inequality or a claim of
global optimality.

Additional experiments use a weighted single-moment proxy and coordinate
searches. A proxy score is explicitly labeled in its result file and is
never treated as a certified tail. In
\texttt{code/coordinate\_prefix\_search.py}, the conditioned-prime
parameters and the suffix are fixed. For a single-moment terminal cost,
the suffix objective is then linear in the prefix distributions and
moments; one adjoint evaluates the prefix coordinate trials. The final
full calculation checks that identity numerically. The program refuses
to apply this linearity shortcut to the nonlinear stratified terminal
objective. These are local searches, not global optimality certificates.

For completeness, its early pullback includes the dependence of the
conditioned probabilities on the early parameters. At the end of the
prefix, let $P,M,R,C,U,\mu$ denote the propagated distributions and moments,
and let $\rho_n$ denote a conditioned-state probability. The late reverse
pass computes a local first variation of the form
\[
 dJ=-d\mu+\langle\lambda_P,dP\rangle+
 \langle\lambda_M,dM\rangle+\langle\lambda_R,dR\rangle+
 \lambda_C\,dC+\lambda_U\,dU+\sum_n\Psi_n\,d\rho_n.
\]
Here $\Psi_n$ is the accumulated direct derivative of the later conditional
losses with respect to that probability. It is computed using the actual
later product distributions at the current parameters. This term is
necessary: changing an early distortion changes both the propagated state
and the weights in every subsequent conditional correction.
Central differences through the short prefix evaluate this pullback; the
long suffix is differentiated once by the adjoint. Integer verification
does not rely on the accuracy or convergence of these search derivatives.
An optional forward-difference variant uses step $10^{-4}$ to reduce
search cost. The joint-product probability laws are cached when only the
explicitly conditioned prime parameters change; this does not alter them.

When the exact early bound covers all conditioned primes, the exploratory
implementation groups probabilities by their prime-presence mask. For
fixed $n$, all parameter dependence of $\rho_n$ is through that mask,
while $1/n$ is fixed. This reduces repeated probability calculations
without changing the finite probability law. The integer verifier still
uses its separate outward-arithmetic implementation.

\subsection{Including the first two distortion parameters}

 \alpha_2\leq\sum_{r\geq r_2}2^{-r}=2^{1-r_2}.
\]
Thus choosing $\delta_2$ at least this explicit value removes the first

factor. At prime $3$, the value
$3^{1-r_3}/2$, where $r_3=\min\{r:3^r\geq K\}$, is a natural breakpoint
of the exact comparison loss; it does not remove all of that stage's loss.
The finite experiment at $K=14490$ chooses parameters close to
$1/8192$ and $1/13122$. At the $8000$-prime horizon this improves the

\subsection{Recursive residue-cap refinements}
There is another rigorous local inequality which may help improve the
early moments. In a fibre with covered proportion $\alpha$, the BBMST
density relative to uniform measure is at most
\[
 g(\alpha)=\frac1{1-\min(\alpha,\delta)}
 \leq\min\{\nu,1+\nu\alpha\},\qquad \nu=(1-\delta)^{-1}.
\]
For $\alpha\leq\delta$, covered points have density zero and uncovered
points density $(1-\alpha)^{-1}$. For $\alpha>\delta$, both densities
are at most $\nu$. These cases also prove the displayed affine bound.
Consequently a residue modulo $q^a$, $a\geq1$, has \emph{marginal}
probability at most
\[
 q^{-a}\min\{\nu_q,1+\nu_q M_q^{(1)}\}.
\]
For example, the parameters in the $14498$ base certificate give, at $q=643$,
a marginal coefficient below $1.024182902374710$, whereas the usual
sequential conditional coefficient is approximately $1.575931487484709$.
The outward integer calculation is recorded in
\texttt{data/marginal-cap-diagnostic.json}. This is a verified local
inequality, not a new modulus certificate. Multiplying these marginal
coefficients across primes would be unjustified.

A version retaining the earlier event is also valid. Let $F_{i-1}(d)$
bound the probability of every residue modulo the earlier-smooth number
$d$, and write $q=p_i$. For $a\geq1$, a valid recursive upper bound is
\[
 F_i(dq^a)=q^{-a}\min\left\{\nu_iF_{i-1}(d),\,
 F_{i-1}(d)+\nu_i\sum_{r\geq1}\sum_{\substack{m\text{ earlier-smooth}\\mq^r\geq K}}
 q^{-r}F_{i-1}(\operatorname{lcm}(d,m))\right\}.
\]
For exponent zero use prefix preservation. To prove the recursion,
multiply the affine fibre-density bound by the earlier residue indicator,
bound $\alpha_i$ by the union sum, and observe that two earlier residue
events intersect either in the empty set or in one residue modulo their
least common multiple. Using existing bounds for $F_{i-1}$ makes the
positive sum convergent. The finite implementation below retains this
dependence explicitly; the independent-product loss estimates do not
silently substitute marginal coefficients.
For any stage $p=p_i$, valid preceding atom bounds imply
\[
 M_p^{(2)}\leq\frac1{(p-1)^2}
       \sum_{d\text{ earlier-smooth}}\tau(d^2)F_{i-1}(d).
\]
Expand the square of the fibre union sum and group pairs of earlier
moduli by their least common multiple. For a prime exponent $a$, there
are $2a+1$ ordered exponent pairs with maximum $a$, giving $\tau(d^2)$.
The usual product atom bound recovers $T_{i-1}$; the recursive refinement
is no larger. Retaining the minimum-modulus restrictions in that pair
sum can sharpen it further. These are integer-moment bounds on the actual
distorted measure, and cannot be substituted for the comparison-model
Euler moments in the convex-loss identity without an additional argument.

\subsection{Implemented finite recursive atom bounds}
\label{sec:finite-recursive-atoms}
The implementation in \texttt{code/recursive\_atom\_bound.py} now evaluates
this recursion with a finite table and an analytic bound for all omitted
terms. This is a bound for actual residue probabilities; it does not assume
that the actual distorted coordinates are independent.

Fix a table limit $N$ and let
\[
 b(d)=\prod_{q^a\parallel d}\frac{\nu_q}{q^a},\qquad
 h_p(m)=\sum_{r\geq1:\,mp^r\geq K}p^{-r}
       =\frac{p^{1-r_0}}{p-1},
 \quad r_0=\min\{r\geq1:mp^r\geq K\}.
\]
All integers in the following sums are smooth over the primes preceding
$p$. Store an upper bound $F(n)$ for every residue modulo $n\leq N$,
and use the valid bound $b(n)$ outside the table. Set
$e(n)=(b(n)-F(n))_+$ inside the table and zero outside. The infinite sum
needed for the affine recursion has the upper bound
\begin{align*}
 H_p(d)={}&\frac{C b(d)}{p-1}
 \prod_{q^a\parallel d}
 \frac{a+1+1/(q-1)}{1+\nu_q/(q-1)}\\
 &-\sum_{m<(K/p)}\left(\frac1{p-1}-h_p(m)\right)
                    b(\operatorname{lcm}(d,m))\\
 &-\sum_{\substack{m\leq N\\\operatorname{lcm}(d,m)\leq N}}
       h_p(m)e(\operatorname{lcm}(d,m)).
\end{align*}
The first line is an exact Euler product for the unrestricted sum over
$m$. The second removes exactly the forbidden powers $mp^r<K$.
The third subtracts only known finite improvements. No infinite remainder
is discarded. The identity
$b(\operatorname{lcm}(d,m))=b(d)b(m)/b(\gcd(d,m))$
is used to evaluate the finite terms.

For each earlier-smooth $d$ with $dp\leq N$, and every $a\geq1$ with
$dp^a\leq N$, update
\[
 F(dp^a)\ \leq\ \frac1{p^a}
       \min\{\nu_p F(d),\ F(d)+\nu_p H_p(d)\}.
\]
The old table is read throughout this update, and all new entries are
inserted together. Entries not involving $p$ are preserved. In the
integer implementation the positive product is rounded upwards and each
subtracted quantity downwards; in particular $e(n)$ is replaced by
$\max\{0,b(n)^--F(n)^+\}$. Taking the minimum with $b(dp^a)^+$ gives
another valid upper bound. This proves the table by induction.

An additional valid density envelope is
\[
 \frac1{1-\min(\alpha,\delta)}
 \leq 1+\alpha+\nu\alpha^2,\qquad 0\leq\alpha\leq1.
\]
For $\alpha\leq\delta$, use
$1/(1-\alpha)=1+\alpha+\alpha^2/(1-\alpha)$.
For $\alpha\geq\delta$, the right side is increasing and equals $\nu$
at $\alpha=\delta$. Thus one may also take the minimum with
$p^{-a}\{F(d)+H_p(d)+\nu_p H_{2,p}(d)\}$. A convenient, deliberately
unrestricted bound for its last term is
\[
 H_{2,p}(d)=\frac{T b(d)}{(p-1)^2}
 \prod_{q^a\parallel d}
 \frac{(a+1)^2+(2a+3)/(q-1)+2/(q-1)^2}
      {1+\nu_q(3q-1)/(q-1)^2}.
\]
This is the exact product-atom sum over two unrestricted earlier moduli
intersected with the residue modulo $d$. For a prime occurring to exponent
$a$ in $d$, the exponent pairs below $a$ contribute $(a+1)^2$, and the
remaining geometric series gives the other two terms. Omitting the
minimum-modulus restrictions only increases this nonnegative sum.
The witness field \texttt{recursive\_density\_degree=2} enables this
additional candidate; degree one retains the affine envelope alone.

To retain the minimum-modulus restriction in the moments, define
\[
 c_{r,p}(n)=\sum_{\substack{m_1,\ldots,m_r\mid n\\
                   \operatorname{lcm}(m_1,\ldots,m_r)=n}}
                  \prod_{j=1}^r h_p(m_j)\ \geq0.
\]
These weights are evaluated by divisor inversion of
$\bigl(\sum_{m\mid n}h_p(m)\bigr)^r$. Expansion of the actual fibre
union bound and the residue-intersection bound gives
\[
 M_p^{(r)}\leq M_{p,\mathrm{old}}^{(r)}
       -\sum_{n\leq N} c_{r,p}(n)e(n),\qquad r=1,2,
\]
where $M_{p,\mathrm{old}}^{(r)}$ is the existing restricted product-atom
majorant. Nonnegativity permits the omitted improvements to be ignored.
The verifier takes the minimum of the resulting first-moment loss,
second-moment loss, and the existing stop-loss candidates. In particular,
it never inserts this improved actual first moment into an identity for
the independent comparison distribution.

\texttt{code/test\_recursive\_atoms.py} compares $1056$ computed caps
with an independent exact-rational recursion, checks $7336$ actual residue
probabilities in explicitly constructed finite distorted measures, and
checks $64$ moment bounds. The finite measures use varying residue choices
and distortion parameters. These tests check the implementation; the
inductive argument above establishes the general inequality.

At cutoff $10000$, the fixed-parameter experiments with $N=2048$ and
$N=16384$ are recorded in
\texttt{data/recursive-atoms-10000.json} and
\texttt{data/recursive-atoms-large-10000.json}. The latter yields up to
approximately $4.921\%$ improvement in the first-moment majorant and
$0.228\%$ in the second, through prime $859$. The moment enclosures use
outward integer arithmetic. The separate comparison files show that
neither improved moment beats the already available conditioned stop-loss
bound at those parameters. That comparison is experimental and is not
an obstruction theorem for other parameters or stronger recursions.

\subsection{Mixing actual moments with comparison losses}
There is a valid way to combine these two kinds of bounds without confusing
the actual and comparison measures. Suppose $A_r\geq\E[\alpha^r]$ and
$G(t)\geq\E[(\alpha-t)_+]$, where $G$ is supplied by the independent
comparison and its conditional evaluation. For $0\leq c<1$ and $x\geq0$,
\[
 (x-\delta)_+\leq c x+(1-c)
                \left(x-\frac{\delta}{1-c}\right)_+.
\]
This follows by splitting at $\delta$ and $\delta/(1-c)$. Consequently
\[
 \Pp_i(B_i)\leq
 \frac{c A_1+(1-c)G(\delta/(1-c))}{1-\delta}.
\]
Likewise, for $\delta\leq t\leq2\delta$,
\[
 (x-\delta)_+\leq\frac{t-\delta}{t^2}x^2+
                   \left(\frac{2\delta}{t}-1\right)(x-t)_+.
\]
On $[\delta,t]$ the difference between the quadratic and $x-\delta$
is nonnegative, with roots $t$ and $\delta t/(t-\delta)\geq t$.
Above $t$ the difference is $(t-\delta)(x-t)^2/t^2$; below $\delta$
nonnegativity suffices. The case $t=\delta$ is immediate.
Both coefficients are nonnegative, so this gives
\[
 \Pp_i(B_i)\leq\frac{(t-\delta)A_2/t^2+
                      (2\delta/t-1)G(t)}{1-\delta}.
\]
Thus improved actual moments can enter a valid inequality involving the
comparison stop-loss, even though they cannot replace its mean in an
identity. These inequalities require $\delta>0$ in the second formula.

The exploratory implementation \texttt{code/explore\_mixed\_atom\_loss.py}
checks $1312$ exact-rational scalar cases and samples threshold grids at
primes $31,53,79,101,151,251,557,829,859$, using the quadratic recursive
moment profile at cutoff $10000$. No mixture improves the original
comparison estimate at these sampled parameters. The floating evaluation
is recorded separately in \texttt{data/mixed-atom-loss-10000.json}; it is
not used in the certified modulus calculation and is not an exhaustive
search over parameters.

\subsection{One-pass fine-prefix verification}
A single verification can retain the benefit of the finer early bins.
Propagate coarse and fine product distributions from the same initial law
with the same rational distortion parameters. Use the fine loss estimates
through a specified index $h$, then use the independently propagated coarse
distribution for every later finite loss and the terminal stratified tail.
The conditioned remainder distribution is also propagated in both
resolutions. Neither distribution is obtained by converting the other's
bins. Every individual loss bound therefore remains valid, and their sum
is a valid survivor bound. The terminal tail uses the coarse distribution
with its original rounding and support bounds.

The fields \texttt{prefix\_index} and \texttt{prefix\_resolution} select
this calculation in \texttt{code/verify\_sparse\_conditioning.py}.
\texttt{code/test\_hybrid\_prefix.py} checks that the resulting integer
survivor equals the independently computed coarse survivor minus its
coarse prefix plus the fine prefix, exactly, including recursive atom
candidates. This avoids requiring three complete component reports for
future certificates while preserving the earlier composition proof.

\subsection{Explicitly controlled exponent truncation}
The exponent cutoff is also a numerical parameter. With witness field
\texttt{product\_tail\_power}$=t$, choose the smallest $H$ satisfying
$p^H\geq2\cdot10^t$ in each product-distribution update, and retain
exponents $0,\ldots,H-1$. The omitted probability at that step is at most
$\nu_p/p^H$. Add this quantity, rounded upwards, to the existing
omitted-exponent error counter. The complement is bounded by its threshold,
so its omitted contribution is bounded exactly as in the original
binning proof. Omitted terms in a lower correction or retained lower
moment are nonnegative and may be discarded. The overflow moment in the
stratified tail still bounds every discarded path.

The original setting is $t=32$; the new verifier permits $12\leq t\leq32$.
The setting $t=20$ reduces the number of retained exponents while leaving
an explicitly enclosed remainder. All arithmetic still uses scale $10^{40}$.
Since these cutoffs never increase $H$, the existing $109$-operations
rounding estimate remains valid. Twelve independent rational recursion
checks, including deliberately much larger omitted probabilities, are
in \texttt{code/test\_product\_truncation.py}.

\subsection{Rechecking a common future distortion}
The terminal report also stores the moment bound for each group of retained
bins assigned to one moment order. If $s_j$ is that order, $b_j>0$ its
old tail coefficient, and $A_j$ the sum of its rounded bin costs, then
\[
 \E[Y^{s_j}\mathbf1_{G_j}]\leq Q_j:=A_j/b_j.
\]
The stored $Q_j$ is rounded upwards. Therefore, for any new \emph{common}
future distortion $d$ satisfying the analytic tail hypotheses,
\[
 \mathcal T_k(d)\leq\sum_j b_{s_j}(d)Q_j+
 \min_s b_s(d)\bigl(U_k^{(s),+}-L_{k,\mathrm{ret}}^{(s),-}\bigr)_+.
\]
This compressed version keeps the group assignment fixed, and uses the
overflow moments already enclosed in the completed base verification. All future primes
use the same $d$; this is not a choice of different future distortion
parameters on different comparison bins. The program
\texttt{code/refine\_tail\_parameter.py} recomputes these coefficients
and sums in outward integer arithmetic. It requires the base witness and
its separately completed report, whose hashes are checked. Its own run
does not replay the finite sieve. The report also saves the exact integer terminal bin state, including its
mass and first-moment rounding bounds. When that state is available, the
program rebuilds each per-bin moment enclosure once and reselects the least
moment order in every bin for each tested common distortion. This is exactly
the stratified-tail inequality already proved above. As a consistency check,
reassembly at the original distortion must reproduce the original
stratified-tail sum exactly. Eighteen exact-rational comparisons check
reselection with changed coefficients, in addition to the existing group
checks. The distortion parameter remains common to all bins.
A finite grid search certifies each
listed candidate, not a global optimum over the continuous parameter.

\subsection{A rigorously accelerated block tail}\label{sec:blocktail}
A longer finite horizon can be enclosed without updating every product
bin at every prime. This refinement begins from a separately completed
certificate at $(k_0,P_0)$ and its stored integer bin state. All future
distortions equal one common rational number $d$. Set $\nu=(1-d)^{-1}$.
For a future comparison exponent $E_q$,
\[
 \Pr(E_q\geq a)=\frac{\nu}{q^a}
 \leq\left(\frac{\nu}{q}\right)^a,\qquad a\geq1.
\]
It is therefore stochastically dominated by a geometric variable $G_q$
on the nonnegative integers with parameter $\rho_q=\nu/q$. Since
$E_q+1\leq2^{E_q}$, the future divisor-count multiplier is dominated by
$2^{Z_b}$, where $Z_b=\sum_{P_0<q\leq p_b}G_q$ and these variables are
independent of the comparison history through $P_0$.

The distribution of $Z_b$ is enclosed using positive power sums:
\[
 A_j=\sum_{P_0<q\leq p_b}\rho_q^j,\qquad h_0=1,\qquad
 h_j=\frac1j\sum_{a=1}^j A_a h_{j-a},\qquad
 \pi_j=\Pr(Z_b=j)=h_j\prod_{P_0<q\leq p_b}(1-\rho_q).
\]
These identities follow by differentiating
$\prod_q(1-\rho_q z)^{-1}$ as a formal power series. For an exponent
cutoff $H$, all omitted first moment is bounded using the exact product
\[
 R_1=\E[2^{Z_b}]=\prod_q\frac{1-\rho_q}{1-2\rho_q},\qquad
 R_{1,>H}=R_1-\sum_{j=0}^H2^j\pi_j.
\]
The implementation uses an upper bound for $R_1$ and lower bounds for
the subtracted terms. All denominators are positive here.

For a block of $m$ consecutive future primes whose first prime is $p_a$
and whose last prime is $p_b$, an upper bound for its total loss,
conditional on initial divisor-count product $y$, is
\[
 L_B(y)=\frac{m}{(p_a-1)(1-d)}
 \left\{\sum_{j=0}^H\pi_j\bigl(y2^j-d(p_a-1)\bigr)_+
                          +yR_{1,>H}\right\}.
\]
Indeed, at each stage in this block the preceding future multiplier is
stochastically at most $2^{Z_b}$, and replacing its denominator $p-1$
by $p_a-1$ only increases the loss. The exponent law here includes
\emph{every} prime from $P_0$ to the current block endpoint; it is not
reset at block boundaries. The first-moment remainder bounds every
omitted exponent, rather than dropping its loss.

The function $L_B$ is convex. Its chord on each original bin $[b,u]$
therefore integrates to an upper bound using the stored probability and
first-moment enclosures. Sum these chord bounds over all blocks up to a
new auxiliary endpoint $(k_1,P_1)$. No initial bin is reinterpreted as a
point mass, and the stored mass and moment rounding errors are retained.

For the infinite part after $P_1$, write $a=2^s$ and
$\rho_* = \max_{P_0<q\leq P_1}\rho_q$. The geometric envelope satisfies
\[
 \E[2^{sZ}] =\prod_q\frac{1-\rho_q}{1-a\rho_q}
 \leq\exp\!\left(\frac{(a-1)\sum_q\rho_q}{1-a\rho_*}\right),
 \qquad a\rho_*<1.
\]
To prove this, expand the logarithm and use
$(a^j-1)/j\leq(a-1)a^{j-1}$. Multiply this growth bound by the previously
proved fractional tail coefficient at $(k_1,P_1)$ and by the initial
bin's moment bound. The moment order may again be selected separately
in each bin. Positive Taylor terms with a geometric remainder enclose
the exponential in integer arithmetic.

The paths omitted from the \emph{initial} retained distribution are
handled separately for their entire future, using the original
fractional-moment coefficient at $(k_0,P_0)$ with the new common $d$ and
the certified omitted moment. Thus retained finite blocks, retained
infinite remainder, and omitted initial paths cover every contribution.
This proves the block-tail estimate without a new assumption on the
actual distorted measure.

\texttt{code/block\_tail\_bound.py} implements this calculation with
outward integer arithmetic. Its report depends explicitly on the hash
of the completed base report. The finite base computation is not replayed
by the block-tail command. The separate test checks $108$ rational
probability enclosures, $16$ actual-loss comparisons, nine chord bounds,
four exponential enclosures and a moment-growth bound. Focused
integration checks also exercise prefix composition and exact terminal
state replay; see \texttt{data/block-tail-integration-checks.json}.
